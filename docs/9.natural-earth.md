# Natural Earth

> The calibrated landcover/landuse vector ground beneath the rest of the map.

## What "natural earth" means here

In this project **natural earth** is the calibrated landcover/landuse **vector** ground — the fills the OpenMapTiles schema draws for grass, wood, rock, sand, farmland, heath, built-up landuse and the like. It is the ground truth that sits beneath the ground raster: the raster composites over it rather than replacing it, so where the imagery is absent, stale or simply too coarse, the vector ground still carries the map.

> [!NOTE]
> Natural earth here is **not** the Natural Earth dataset and **not** a shaded-relief raster. The basemap's former `ne2_shaded` relief raster is no longer inherited by the style; relief now comes from the Mapterhorn DEM hillshade (see [Elevation & Terrain](5.dem.md)).

The band is recoloured by [`applyNaturalEarthPalette()`](../scripts/build.mjs) — the landcover/landuse half of the `TERRAIN_PALETTE` toggle — while the earthy canvas floor beneath it is recoloured by [`applyCorePalette()`](../scripts/build.mjs) from `COLOURS.TERRAIN.BACKGROUND`. Both read their values from the central `COLOURS` object (`COLOURS.TERRAIN`, `COLOURS.LANDCOVER`, `COLOURS.LANDUSE`).

### The natural-earth palette

[`applyNaturalEarthPalette()`](../scripts/build.mjs) maps each basemap landcover/landuse layer family to one `COLOURS` key:

| Basemap layer family                                                                          | `COLOURS` key                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Grass, meadow, garden, recreation ground, cemetery, wetland/swamp, marsh, allotments, stadium | `COLOURS.TERRAIN.GRASS`         |
| Wood, forest, mangrove                                                                        | `COLOURS.TERRAIN.WOOD`          |
| Rock, scree, bare rock                                                                        | `COLOURS.LANDCOVER.ROCK`        |
| Sand, dune, beach                                                                             | `COLOURS.TERRAIN.SAND`          |
| Farmland, farm, orchard and vineyard                                                          | `COLOURS.LANDCOVER.FARMLAND`    |
| Heath, scrub                                                                                  | `COLOURS.LANDCOVER.HEATH`       |
| Residential                                                                                   | `COLOURS.TERRAIN.RESIDENTIAL`   |
| Military                                                                                      | `COLOURS.LANDUSE.MILITARY`      |
| Quarry                                                                                        | `COLOURS.LANDUSE.QUARRY`        |
| Glacier                                                                                       | `COLOURS.TERRAIN.ICE`           |
| Glacier outline (the outline **line**)                                                        | `COLOURS.WATER.GLACIER_OUTLINE` |

The glacier outline is the non-obvious reuse: its line colour lives in the **water** palette even though the fill it edges is terrain. The `Background` canvas floor is recoloured outside this function by `applyCorePalette()` from `COLOURS.TERRAIN.BACKGROUND`, and `applyParkDifferentiation()` also recolours the `Park` fill.

**Calibrated** here means these fills, and the canvas floor beneath them, are matched to the reference imagery so the vector ground and the raster read as a single surface; see [Ground Raster](11.ground-raster.md#re-tuning-the-ground) for the stops and levers.

## Where this band sits

Natural earth is the `natural-earth` band in [`RENDER_STACK`](../scripts/build.mjs), above the `core` canvas floor. Its calibrated fills sit beneath the `ground-raster` band, while the park boundaries and labels sit above it. When the satellite ground is enabled the imagery composites over the fills rather than replacing them. See [Render Stack](10.render-stack.md) for the band table and the step model.
